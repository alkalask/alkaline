Can the sea hold more CO2?

The ocean has quietly taken in 29% of the CO2 we emit. Since 2016 it has stopped taking more.

Adding alkalinity lets the surface absorb more. Field trials have begun, one of them traced through open water with pink dye.

The carbon can stay dissolved as bicarbonate for 10,000 years or more. Whether that is safe at scale is the research COP31 can fund.Whether that is safe at scale is the research governments can still fund.Whether that is safe at scale is what publicly funded research can settle.

COP31 · Antalya · 9 to 20 November 2026 Ask your delegation to fund the research

In August 2025, scientists on a research ship released alkalinity and pink dye into the Gulf of Maine and tracked the patch for five days with gliders . So far, no significant change in plankton or larvae.3

The ocean sink has stalled

The ocean absorbs nearly a third of the CO2 we emit, and nobody asked it to. That uptake has been flat since 2016, which the Global Carbon Budget puts largely down to climate variability.1 Ocean alkalinity enhancement, or OAE, asks one practical question: can we safely raise what the sea is able to hold? In theory the answer could reach billions of tonnes a year.13

29percent of the CO2 people emitted over the past decade was taken up by the ocean1

Add

Add alkalinity to surface water. It can come from ground minerals such as olivine, or from a hydroxide solution made with electricity.

Pictured: natural olivine sand at Papakōlea, Hawaiʻi, weathering into the sea.

Draw

The treated water holds its carbon as bicarbonate, so over weeks to months the surface draws more CO2 down from the air above it.

Pictured: the Gulf of Maine from space, June 2025, two months before the trial there. NASA.

Measure

Ships, gliders, drifters and satellites track where the alkalinity goes and how much carbon follows. This is the hard part, and the part that needs funding.

Pictured: R/V Atlantis of Woods Hole Oceanographic Institution, 2016.

Two ways to add alkalinity

CO2 + H2O ⇌ H+ + HCO3−

Alkalinity removes the hydrogen ions shown in pink. The balance shifts to the right, and the same water can hold more carbon as stable bicarbonate.2

Mineral

How
Grind alkaline rock such as olivine, basalt or limestone and spread it in coastal or open water.
Strength
It speeds up the natural weathering that has regulated ocean chemistry for millions of years.
Open questions
Slow dissolution, trace metals such as nickel and chromium, particles that affect filter feeders, and the footprint of mining.6

Electrochemical

How
Split seawater or brine into an alkaline stream that returns to the sea and an acid stream handled on land.
Strength
No mining, already dissolved, and dosed precisely. The Gulf of Maine trial used purified sodium hydroxide.3
Open questions
Energy demand that only makes sense with clean power, and safe use or disposal of the acid.

How long the carbon stays

Typical storage times from the literature. Each step to the right is ten times longer. Carbon held as bicarbonate sits near the top of the scale.24

Durable is not the same as verified. Showing that a specific tonne was removed is still the central research problem. It is also conditional: add too much alkalinity in one place and carbonate minerals can form, which undoes part of the gain.5

Field trials, done in the open

In the Gulf of Maine trial, the team checked bacteria, phytoplankton, dying cells, photosynthetic health, chlorophyll, zooplankton, fish larvae and lobster larvae. None showed a significant difference. Fish, whales and fisheries were not measured.3

Who pays so far

Governments fund this research, but thinly, and in the United States that money was cut in 2025. Meanwhile one of the main funders also campaigns for the field, and companies are already buying removal that is still hard to verify. That is why the ask is for public money and independent review.

Philanthropy
The Carbon to Sea Initiative launched in 2023 with $50 million from a group of foundations to fund OAE research. It also campaigns for public funding and clearer permitting, so it is a funder and an advocate at once.1621
Credit buyers
In 2025 buyers including Stripe, Google and Shopify agreed to pay $31.3 million for 115,211 tons of removal from one OAE company between 2026 and 2030. That buys future removal. It is not a research grant.17
Public money
It exists, but it is thin. US federal investment in research on all ocean carbon removal methods came to nearly $61.5 million across 2023 and 2024, by Carbon to Sea’s count.21 Federal ocean science was then cut, and by August 2026 that backing had “nearly dried up”, a Pulitzer Center report found.22 Congress set aside $10 million for 2026.23 The National Academies put the research need for OAE alone at $125 to $200 million.2 The Gulf of Maine trial was funded in part by two private donors and in part by a federal agency.15

The risks, stated plainly

Verification

Proving that a specific tonne was removed, and telling it apart from the sea’s natural swings, is still the hardest open problem in OAE.510

The research group CarbonPlan puts confidence in verifying the electrochemical route at 3 out of 5.18

Ecology

Local pH spikes, trace metals from minerals, particles and shifts in plankton. Lab effects depend on dose and exposure time.6

Governance

Water crosses borders. The London Protocol’s geoengineering amendment covers only ocean fertilization and is not yet in force.7

Equity

Who benefits, who carries the risk and who decides. Coastal and Indigenous communities need a real say inreal power over trials near them.6

Moral hazard

Less pressure to cut emissions. Visible investment in removal could weaken the case for cutting them. Any framework has to face that directly.2

To our delegation at COP31,To our delegation,

Over the past decade the ocean has taken up about 29% of the carbon dioxide people emitted. Since 2016 that uptake has stalled, which the Global Carbon Budget puts largely down to climate variability. We are leaning on a sink that is no longer keeping pace.

Ocean alkalinity enhancement (OAE) may let the sea safely absorb more, and hold the carbon as dissolved bicarbonate for 10,000 years or more. The evidence is early. In 2025 the first field trial permitted by the US Environmental Protection Agency released alkalinity in the Gulf of Maine. Preliminary results found no significant change in the plankton, fish larvae and lobster larvae they measured. The same team says effects on fisheries are still uncertain, and that proving how much carbon was removed needs more work.

That is why public research has to come first. I ask you to:

1. Back public funding for independent, openly published research on monitoring, reporting and verification, counting the full life cycle.

2. Support agreed ecological thresholds and independent monitoring for every trial.

3. Clarify how research trials are permitted, at home and under international ocean law, and list every trial in a public register.

4. Involve coastal and Indigenous communities and rights holders from the start.

5. State plainly that OAE adds to emission cuts and never replaces them.

None of this commits anyone to deployment. It commits us to finding out, in the open.

That is why public research has to come before commercial scale. I ask you to support a COP31 outcome that:I ask you to back national funding and international action, including at COP32, that:

1. Funds openly published research on monitoring, reporting and verification that counts the full life cycle, with funders disclosed and review by people with no commercial stake in OAE.

2. Sets ecological thresholds that pause a trial automatically.

3. Clarifies jurisdiction, liability and consent under UNCLOS and the London Protocol, and lists every trial in a public register.

4. Gives coastal and Indigenous communities real decision power over trials near them.

5. States plainly that OAE adds to emission cuts and never replaces them, and keeps it out of carbon credits and net zero claims until removal is independently verified.

None of this commits anyone to deployment. It commits us to finding out, in the open, before the market decides for us.

Questions delegates ask

Is this geoengineering?

It is marine carbon dioxide removal that deliberately changes seawater chemistry, so treaty bodies treat it as marine geoengineering. London Protocol parties list it among four techniques for priority evaluation.7

Is this funded by oil companies?

Not that we can find. The named funders of the Carbon to Sea Initiative are foundations, and the announced credit buyers are technology and consumer companies. The greenwashing risk lies elsewhere: credits sold before removal can be verified, or bought as a substitute for cutting emissions.1617

Is it ready to deploy?

No. Project Drawdown lists OAE under Keep Watching, not among the solutions ready to use. Lab studies have found some minerals raise alkalinity less than expected, and the field trials so far are small.1920

Why COP31?Why now, after COP31?

COP31 closed in Antalya on 20 November 2026, and national research budgets and COP32 come next. Is it legal to run field trials?

National permits apply. The Gulf of Maine trial needed an EPA permit and 75 days of public comment. Internationally, the London Protocol amendment formally covers only ocean fertilization and is not yet in force. In 2025 the parties advanced criteria for assessing OAE research.38

Will it harm marine life?

Lab studies show effects that depend on concentration and exposure time. The Gulf of Maine trial found no significant differences in plankton or larvae, but it did not measure fish, mammals or fisheries. Mineral routes add the risk of trace metals. That is why the ask includes guardrails.36

How would anyone know a tonne was removed?

Teams measure the alkalinity they add, track pH and CO2 pressure in the treated water, and use ocean models to estimate how much CO2 crosses the surface over the following months. A credible claim also subtracts the emissions from mining, grinding, shipping and power, and any carbon that escapes again. Natural variability is large, so this is where public research money is most needed.510

Could OAE be used for greenwashing?

Yes, if it is sold as an offset for emissions that continue, or if credits and net zero claims arrive before the removal is verified. No tonne should be counted twice. The fifth ask makes OAE explicitly additional to emission cuts, and credible verification has to come before any credit is issued.2The fifth ask makes OAE explicitly additional to emission cuts, and research funding should stay separate from offset claims until verification is solved.2

What would the funding pay for?

Open verification methods and shared data, independently monitored field trials, long term ecological studies, and governance pilots led by coastal communities.5

Can it reverse ocean acidification?

Locally, yes. Added alkalinity raises pH where it is applied. At research scale the effect is small and temporary, so it should be studied as a benefit and never promised as a cure.92
